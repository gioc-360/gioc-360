## Projects

### Geniie 🎧 — Desktop Music Library, DJ Engine & iPod Manager
*Personal App · Rust, Tauri, SQLite*

https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/060578e3-aedb-47ef-81a0-5054320613b5

Geniie is a Mac app I built because I wanted a music player that can connect to my iPod and has AI-native features to help me discover music I haven't heard before, old and new. The app scans your library, repairs metadata against AcoustID and MusicBrainz, and persists everything via SQLite. It has a DJ engine with beat and key detection, harmonic compatibility on the Camelot wheel, energy-arc sequencing, and stem separation so a transition can lay vocals over the next track's instrumental, mimicking how a DJ auto-mixes a set. Geniie helps organize your iPod so it's clean, has complete cover art, and syncs zero-bloat playlists ready for offline listening.

### AI Operations Capacity Framework — Independent Study with Prof. Manzoor
*Fall 2026 · In progress*

I'm working with Professor Emaad Manzoor on a framework for how companies should allocate a fixed AI budget so that every dollar buys actual, useful work: which systems to adopt, whether to buy dedicated hardware or rent cloud capacity, and what that looks like operationally. It takes a classic operations management question traditionally asked about factories, how much useful output a unit of capacity generates and what each unit costs, and applies it to modern AI infrastructure. Because demand for AI spikes unpredictably, expensive compute sits idle outside peak windows, and leading firms are quickly hitting electricity and power walls rather than pure hardware limits. This project builds on the cost-visibility and resource-allocation problems I tackled at Dell. Still in progress; chapters not yet public.

### Kokosuki ここすき 🇯🇵 — Japanese trainer
*Personal App · JavaScript, PWA · [live](https://renshu-iota.vercel.app)*

Kokosuki is a practice app for hiragana, katakana, and N5 vocabulary, and I use it to practice my Japanese. Every Japanese app I tried was built around some gimmick, like streak counters or mascot characters you level up. I just wanted to learn enough words to understand Japanese song lyrics and poetry. The app runs on FSRS, a memory-modeling algorithm that predicts when you're about to forget a specific card, instead of the older SM-2, the fixed-interval algorithm most flashcard apps, including Anki, still default to. It's offline-first and built as a PWA, a progressive web app that installs like a native app straight from the browser, so it still works even when I'm on a flight.

### Kiru 斬 — Booth game for the Johnson Japan Club
*Johnson Japan Club · Node.js, WebSockets, Canvas*

https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/7cdbe041-5a0c-4449-841f-16f9d1453ce4

JJC was one of the smaller clubs at Johnson, and a small budget made it hard to run events people would pick over the ones clubs with much larger budgets were putting on the same weeks. The way out was selling higher-priced events, since every ticket grew both the budget and the membership, so the whole problem came down to getting people to stop at our booth during club fairs long enough to buy. I built Kiru to make stopping the fun part. A visitor scans a QR code on the laptop and their own phone becomes the blade for a 21-second round of slicing Japanese food, dodging bombs and hacking at a bento boss, while the big screen replays every slash for the line waiting behind them. Kiru was one of the ways I marketed our events by driving engagement at the booth, and with membership growing and tickets selling out, our yearly budget grew 367%, from $750 to $3,500, and our operating funds grew 75%, from $400 to $700.
